## What dispatch software is for

Strip away the feature pages and dispatch software has one job: get every ride from "booked" to "paid" without anyone dropping it. For a small operator that path looks the same every time.

1. A ride comes in, from your website, a phone call or a corporate client.
2. Someone assigns a vehicle and a driver.
3. The driver gets the pickup details on their phone.
4. If it's an airport ride, the pickup follows the flight.
5. The rider hears from you at each step.
6. The ride is paid, and the record is there when you need it.

Anything that makes one of those six steps faster or harder to get wrong is worth paying for. Anything that doesn't is a feature you'll never open.

## The six things it has to do well

**Take the booking in one place.** Rides from your website, from the phone and from corporate accounts should all land on the same board. If online bookings live in one tool and phone bookings in a notebook, you'll double-book a car eventually.

**Assign without conflicts.** When you put a driver or a vehicle on a ride, the system should warn you if they're already booked at that time. That one warning is worth more than any report.

**Give the driver everything on their phone.** The pickup address, the passenger, the flight, the notes and a way to mark the trip en route, arrived, picked up and completed. If drivers have to call dispatch for details, the software isn't doing its job.

**Follow the flight.** Airport work is most of the money for most operators. The trip should carry the flight number and show its status, so a late landing doesn't leave a chauffeur waiting at the wrong time or a rider waiting at the curb.

**Keep the rider informed without a call.** A confirmation, a reminder the day before and a text when the driver is on the way. Riders who know where their car is don't call you at 5am to ask.

**Get paid.** Card on file, deposits, payment links for rides booked by phone, and refunds when plans change. The money should land in your own account.

## What you can skip

Big dispatch platforms are built for fleets of fifty and up, and their price reflects it. If you run one to twenty cars, you can usually skip:

- **Shuttle and per-seat modules,** unless you run scheduled shuttles.
- **Farm-in and farm-out networks,** unless you regularly hand rides to other operators.
- **Multi-location management,** unless you have more than one base.
- **A branded passenger app.** Riders book a car a few times a year; most won't download an app for it. A booking page that works well on a phone does the same job.

## Where dispatch software stops

Here is the part most comparisons leave out. Dispatch software runs the ride once it exists. It doesn't bring the ride in.

The booking still has to happen somewhere, and for most operators that's their website. Many dispatch systems give you a booking widget to paste into a site you already have, or a booking page on their own domain. Either way, you end up paying for three things that don't talk to each other well: the website, the dispatch system and whatever you use to show up on Google.

When you compare options, ask where the rider actually books, under whose name, and whether the website is part of the price or a separate bill.

## How to compare the prices

The monthly number on a pricing page is rarely what you pay. Before you sign, get answers to these in writing:

- **What grows with my business?** Some systems price by the number of trips you run, some by users or vehicles, some by a flat monthly rate. A price that grows with your trips is a tax on your good months.
- **What's the setup fee?** One-time fees from a few hundred dollars upward are common.
- **What do card payments cost?** If the payments run through the software company, ask for the exact rate. A difference of half a percent on $30,000 of rides a month is $150 a month.
- **Which features are add-ons?** The passenger app, text messaging, the website and marketing tools are often extra.
- **How do I leave?** Ask for the notice period and whether you can export your customer list and trip history.
