## What limo insurance costs per vehicle

Limo insurance is commercial auto insurance for vehicles that carry paying passengers, and it costs far more than a personal policy on the same vehicle. Brokers commonly quote **$5,000 to $10,000 per vehicle per year** for a sedan or SUV, or roughly $450 to $833 a month, according to [Insurify](https://insurify.com/business-insurance/commercial-auto/limo/). A personal policy on a similar vehicle rarely tops $2,000 a year.

Prices have been climbing. In the same figures, 87% of operators said their premium went up over three years, and the most common increase was 10 to 15%. Budget for a rise at every renewal, not a flat number.

## What a policy covers

A limo policy is usually several coverages bundled together:

- **Commercial auto liability.** Injury and damage you cause to other people. This is the part regulators set minimums for, and the biggest part of the premium.
- **Physical damage.** Repairs or replacement for your own vehicle after a crash, theft or storm. Deductibles commonly run from $500 to $5,000.
- **Uninsured and underinsured motorist.** Covers you and your passengers when the other driver has no insurance or not enough.
- **Hired and non-owned auto.** Covers vehicles you rent or affiliate drivers' cars used for your business.
- **General liability.** Incidents that don't involve driving, such as a passenger tripping at your office.

## The minimums: federal and state

If you carry passengers for hire across state lines, the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration sets the floor. You need interstate operating authority and at least **$1.5 million** in liability coverage for vehicles designed to carry 15 or fewer people including the driver, and **$5 million** for 16 or more, per the [FMCSA](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/safety/passenger-safety/licensing-and-insurance-requirements-hire-motor-carriers-passengers). The highest-capacity vehicle in the fleet sets the level for the whole fleet.

If you only drive within your state, the state, county or city sets the minimum, and it varies widely. Airports and corporate clients often set their own insurance requirements, which can be higher than the legal minimum. Ask what your airport and your biggest prospects require before you choose a limit.

## What drives your premium

Insurers price each policy on the risk they think it carries. The main factors:

- **Vehicle size and value.** More seats and a more expensive vehicle mean a bigger premium.
- **Coverage limits and deductibles.** A $1.5 million limit costs more than the state minimum; a higher deductible costs less.
- **Drivers.** Age, years of professional experience and driving records. One driver with recent violations can raise the price for the whole fleet.
- **Claims history.** Past claims raise future premiums, often for several years.
- **Where you operate.** Dense cities and states with more lawsuits cost more.
- **Mileage and use.** Long intercity runs and late-night party work carry more risk than daytime airport transfers.

## How to bring the price down

- **Hire carefully, and check records yearly.** Run a motor vehicle record on every driver before hiring and every year after. Insurers reward clean records.
- **Use a broker who specializes in livery.** Not every insurer writes limo policies, and a specialist broker knows which ones do. Get at least three quotes every renewal.
- **Raise the deductible on physical damage** if you can cover the difference from savings.
- **Install dashcams and telematics.** Some insurers give a discount for them, and footage helps settle disputed claims.
- **Pay annually** if you can; monthly payment plans can cost more in total.
- **Keep a written safety program.** Driver training, vehicle inspections and a drug and alcohol policy all count in your favor at underwriting.

## Building insurance into your rates

Insurance is a fixed cost: you pay it whether the vehicle is booked or not. An SUV insured for $7,500 a year costs $625 a month before it earns anything. Spread over 80 booked hours a month, that's about $8 of every billable hour, so it has to be in your rate from day one. Our guide to [limo rates](/journal/limo-rates) walks through building an hourly price from your costs, and the [guide to starting a limo business](/journal/how-to-start-a-limo-business) covers where insurance fits in the order of everything else.

## Conclusion

Plan on $5,000 to $10,000 a year for each sedan or SUV, more for vans and much more for minicoaches, and expect it to rise at renewal. Get quotes from specialist brokers before you buy a vehicle, since the vehicle you choose changes the premium, and build the cost into your rates from the first ride.
