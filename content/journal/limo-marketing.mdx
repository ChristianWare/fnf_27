## Get found first

**1. Finish your Google Business Profile.** For most limo companies, the map listing brings in more riders than the website does. Choose the right primary category, list every service and the areas you serve, add photos of each vehicle inside and out, and post an update every week or two. Our look at [where a limo company's traffic actually comes from](/journal/seo-for-limo-companies) shows why this comes first.

**2. Ask every happy rider for a review.** Reviews move you up the map listing and decide who gets the call. Send the review link in the thank-you message after every completed ride, and have chauffeurs mention it at drop-off. Reply to every review, good or bad, within a day or two. Never pay or offer discounts for reviews; Google removes them and can suspend the listing.

**3. Build a page for each airport and route.** Riders search for "car service to [airport]" and "[city] to [city] car service", and a page that answers exactly that, with your flat rate, the pickup process and a way to book, can rank for it. A page per airport you serve and per intercity run you actually drive beats a long list of thin town pages.

**4. Show your prices and let riders book online.** A rider comparing companies at night is more likely to choose the one that shows a price and confirms the booking on the spot. Publish your airport flat rates and hourly minimums, and take bookings and payment on your own site. See [limo rates](/journal/limo-rates) for setting them.

## Go after the people who book for others

**5. Corporate travel managers and executive assistants.** One account can mean dozens of rides a month. Find the person who books the rides, offer a first ride on your best vehicle and make it flawless. The full process is in [how to get corporate clients](/journal/how-to-get-corporate-clients-limo-company).

**6. Hotel concierges and front desks.** Guests ask the concierge for a car to the airport or dinner. Visit in person, leave cards, and make sure every ride they send goes perfectly; one bad ride ends the referrals.

**7. Wedding planners, venues and funeral homes.** Each one books transportation over and over for clients who need it to go right. Ask to be on their preferred vendor list, and offer to link to them from your site in return.

The [leads tool](/services/leads) finds these businesses in your market every morning, with the decision-maker's contact and a script for the first message.

## Keep the riders you have

**8. Get the website on everything.** The vehicles, the business cards, the receipts, the email signature and the confirmation texts. Riders who rode once and search your name later should land on your site, not a marketplace listing.

**9. Stay in touch.** A short email before the busy seasons (holidays, prom, wedding season, your city's big events) reminds past riders you exist. Keep it to one useful message, not a weekly newsletter.

**10. Plan around the calendar.** Big events bring group bookings: conventions, tournaments, festivals, graduation and prom. Publish a page for each one two or three months ahead with your rates and availability, so it's indexed before riders start searching.

## What to measure

Ask every new rider how they heard about you, and record it with the booking. After three months, you'll know which of these ideas brings in rides and which only brings in visits. Put more time into the first kind.

## Conclusion

The best limo marketing is a strong Google listing, steady reviews, pages that answer what riders search for and a site that lets them book on the spot. Add the people who book rides for others, and keep the riders you already have coming back.
