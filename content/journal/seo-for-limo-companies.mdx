## The site that gets 5,000 visits a month

There is a chauffeured car service in Boston that gets about 5,000 visits a month from Google, with a website that is not much bigger than yours. We exported every search it ranks for and sorted the traffic by where it lands. The split is worth memorizing.

- **54%** comes from the Google Maps listing. The company is in the top three map results for 101 different searches: "boston car service", "limo service boston", "car service to logan airport", and 98 more like them.
- **13%** comes from the homepage, which sits on page one for the same searches right under the map.
- **15%** is people typing the company's name into Google.
- **11%** comes from airport guides and route pages: a terminal map, a guide to one airport's arrivals level, and a page for the Boston to New York run.
- **3%** comes from 93 town pages, one for every suburb within an hour of Boston.
- **4%** is the blog and everything else.

Read that again. The 93 town pages, which took the most work, bring the least traffic. The map listing, which the owner can manage from a phone, brings more than everything else combined.

## Why the map wins

When someone in your city searches "car service near me" or "limo service phoenix", Google shows three map listings before it shows a single website. Those three get the calls. The map ranks businesses by how close they are to the searcher, how complete and active the listing is, and how many recent reviews it has.

None of that is code. It is the category you picked, the photos you uploaded, the questions you answered, and whether you asked the last fifty riders for a review. An operator with 120 recent five-star reviews outranks one with eight, every time, and no website fixes that.

## What the homepage has to do

The homepage is the one page that ranks for the big searches in your city, so it has to say what you do in the words riders use. "Black car, chauffeur and limo service in Phoenix" is a heading that ranks. "Elevate your travel experience" is not, because nobody searches for it.

Put the airport, the city, the services and the prices from on the homepage, with the booking form above the fold. Then make sure no other page on your site competes with it. We see sites where four pages rank for the same search, in positions 18, 54, 67 and 84, and none of them wins. One page per search.

## The pages worth building

After the homepage and the map, the traffic comes from answering the questions riders have before they book. For a Phoenix operator that means a page for each Sky Harbor terminal, the Sky Train, and the cell phone lot, and a page for each real run: Scottsdale to the airport, Phoenix to Sedona, Tucson to Phoenix. Each one gets a price, a drive time, a photo of the actual vehicle, and a booking box.

Fifteen pages like that beat sixty pages that say the same thing with the town name swapped. Google treats the swapped-name pages as doorway pages and can push the whole site down for them.

## Conclusion

If you have one hour a week for SEO, spend it on the map listing: reviews, photos, a post. If you have a developer, have them fix the homepage first and build the airport and route pages second. Town pages come last, and only where you have something real to say about the town. That is the order the traffic arrives in.
